PHASE 4: SAMPLING
=================

Status: COMPLETE. Balanced training and test tables with a 500 m non-fire
buffer. Next: Phase 5 (validation).


1. PURPOSE
----------
Turn the fire events and the predictor layers into labelled sample points: one
row per point, with a label (1 for fire, 0 for non-fire) and the 12 predictor
values at that point. The model is trained and tested on these rows.


2. WHAT WAS DONE
----------------
1. Loaded the fixed layers and the period layers into memory, once each.
2. For each period, kept only the fire events whose location has all 12
   predictors valid.
3. Drew random points inside the Chikangawa boundary, in batches of 200,000,
   with seed 42.
4. Rejected any point within 500 m of a detection from the same period.
5. Rejected any point with a missing predictor.
6. Kept points until the non-fire count equalled the fire count for that period.
7. Assigned each row a 5 km spatial block ID for the cross-validation.
8. Checked that each period is balanced.

Folder: Phase_4_samples/
Script: phase4_sampling.py
Checking script (run first, separately): buffer_coverage.py

5. SETTINGS
-----------
  Random seed:              42
  Non-fire buffer:          500 m, same-period detections only
  Training period:          2002 to 2015
  Test period:              2016 to 2024
  Non-fire to fire ratio:   1:1 per period
  Spatial block size:       5 km
  Fire points:              representative location of each event

7. WHY IT MATTERS
-----------------
- The design is balanced. A model trained on a 1:1 table is not pushed toward
  the majority class.
- The 500 m buffer keeps the non-fire points away from recorded fires. It
  also means the model can partly learn distance to fire from where the samples
  sit. Phase 5 tests this.
- Each period uses only its own detections for the buffer, so no test-period
  fire shapes a training sample.
- The 5 km blocks are stored now, so the cross-validation in Phase 5 can keep
  neighbouring points in the same fold.


8. WHAT THE DISSERTATION SHOULD NOTE
------------------------------------
- Buffer: 500 m, chosen from buffer_coverage.csv before any model result. The
  decision is in the project decisions log (D10).
- Distance bias: the non-fire points are at least 500 m from a detection, so
  the model may partly learn distance to fire. Report the 0, 250 and 1000 m
  sensitivity runs in Phase 5.
- Superseded run: the earlier 1000 m run rejected 95 percent of training
  candidates. Its counts are kept as the extreme sensitivity case.
- Lost fire events: 31 events in training and 13 in testing were dropped
  because a predictor was missing at their location. That is 1.3 and 0.7
  percent. Check where they are before the write-up (see section 9).
- Background area: the non-fire points come from the whole 1,577 km2
  boundary, not only the plantation (about 535 km2). This is an open decision
  (O2 in the decisions log).
- Label noise: MODIS locates fires to about 1 km, so some non-fire points may
  sit on unrecorded fires. This is a limitation of the data, not something the
  sampler can remove.
