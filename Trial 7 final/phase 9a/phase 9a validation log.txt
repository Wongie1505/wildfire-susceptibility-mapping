PHASE 9a: VALIDATION AGAINST RAW FIRMS FIRE POINTS (TRIAL 7)
============================================================

Status: COMPLETE (numbers from the pie charts and the validation tables are
still to be filled in, see sections 7 and 12). Date run: 16 November 2026.

Context. Trial 7 is a declared new analysis. No model was run in this phase.
The map under test is the Phase 7c susceptibility map: the mean 6a model score
over 2016 to 2024, in three classes (Low 0 to 0.34, Medium 0.34 to 0.49,
High 0.49 to 1).


1. PURPOSE
----------
Check the map against fire observations that were not used to build it, in the
same design as the Trial 5 validation: where do fire points fall on the map,
and where do non-fire points fall? A useful map should put most fire points in
the High class and most non-fire points in the Low class.


2. WHAT WAS DONE
----------------
- Took the raw MODIS fire detections with confidence 75 or more (not clustered
  into events) for the validation period 2016 to 2024, and labelled them
  fire = 1.
- Drew random non-fire points inside the forest, the same number as the fire
  points, labelled fire = 0.
- Found the map class under every point.
- Saved all points in one CSV with a fire column.
- Drew two pie charts, one for each group, showing how its points divide
  between the Low, Medium and High classes.
- Drew two overlay maps with the fire hotspot density.


3. HOW IT WAS DONE
------------------
Script: phase9a_validation.py (Spyder).

Step 1. Fire points. File fire_detections_filtered.csv from the Trial 6 fire
  events phase: every raw detection from July to December, confidence 75 or
  more, inside the Chikangawa boundary. Years 2016 to 2024 were kept: 2,981
  detections. Their coordinates were converted to EPSG:32736. 2,973 fall on a
  map cell and were kept. The other 8 lie in border cells that are outside the
  500 m forest mask.
Step 2. Non-fire points. Random points inside the forest boundary, seed 42,
  kept only if they were at least 500 m from every fire point and fell on a map
  cell. 2,973 points were drawn, equal to the number of fire points.
Step 3. A second set of 2,973 non-fire points was drawn with no distance rule.
  It is used only for one AUC value (step 6).
Step 4. Class lookup. Each point takes the class and the mean score of the
  500 m cell it falls in.
Step 5. Outputs. validation_points.csv (point id, fire = 1 or 0, x, y, lon,
  lat, date, mean score, class), class_distribution.csv (counts and share per
  class for each group, with the share of forest area per class), and the two
  pie charts RF_Fire_1_Distribution.png and RF_Fire_0_Distribution.png.
Step 6. AUC of the mean score, fire points against non-fire points, once with
  the 500 m buffer and once without it (validation_summary.csv).
Step 7. Overlay maps. A Gaussian kernel density of the fire points
  (bandwidth factor 0.1, on a 500 m grid, scaled from 0 to 1 and clipped to the
  forest). A hotspot is a density above 0.6. Option A shows the class map next
  to the density map with the points. Option B shows the class map with hatched
  hotspot outlines. The class maps are drawn on the 30.6 m display surface from
  Phase 7c, with the Trial 5 layout, scale bar and data box.


4. WHY EACH CHOICE WAS MADE
---------------------------
- Raw fire points and not events: this repeats the Trial 5 design, and each
  point is a satellite detection that needs no clustering rule to defend.
- Confidence 75 or more: the same filter as in Trial 6, to drop weak
  detections.
- Validation period 2016 to 2024: the map is the mean of the scores of those
  years, so the fire points of the same years are the natural comparison.
- Non-fire points with a buffer: this is the Trial 5 and Trial 6 method and
  allows comparison. The buffer is also known to flatter a map (section 9), so
  a second set without a buffer is used to measure the effect.
- Class lookup on the 500 m map: this is the analysis grid. The 30 m surface is
  for display only (Phase 7c).
- Same colours as the Phase 7c map, so that all Trial 7 figures use one palette.
- FIRMS was not used as a label or a predictor, so this check does not reuse the
  training labels. FIRMS and the burned area product both come from MODIS, so
  the check is independent of the labels but not of the sensor.

What the overlay maps show (visual reading, to be confirmed with the numbers
above):
- The fire density has one dominant hotspot, in the narrow middle of the forest
  on the western side. On the susceptibility map this hotspot lies over Medium
  and Low land, with only its upper edge over High.
- Two small hotspots at the same narrow part, and one in the south west, lie
  over mixed Medium and High land.
- The large High areas in the north and the south of the forest contain
  scattered fire points but no strong hotspot. A hotspot is defined relative to
  the highest density in the forest, so a dominant hotspot lowers the density
  of everything else. The side by side map shows moderate fire density in the
  north and the south east.
- Taken together, the overlay shows partial agreement: the map covers part of
  the fire activity, and it does not capture the strongest hotspot of
  2016 to 2024.

8. CHALLENGES AND FIXES
-----------------------
No technical problems with the script. The layout of the pie chart labels was
corrected before the run, so that labels do not overlap the slices.


9. LIMITATIONS
--------------
- The 500 m buffer favours the map. Non-fire points kept away from fire points
  tend to fall in cells that never burned, and those are the cells that the
  model rates low (the model uses fire history). This is the same artifact that
  made the Trial 6 buffer test look better as the buffer grew. The unbuffered
  AUC is the fairer number.
- A MODIS fire point comes from a pixel of about 1 km, so its position has an
  error of the order of one or two map cells.
- FIRMS and the burned area product both come from MODIS and share limits such
  as cloud, small fires and overpass times.
- The map is the mean of the 2016 to 2024 scores. Its fire history inputs use
  earlier burns, including burns in the same period, so some overlap with the
  fire points remains (Phase 7c). A check with a map built only from 2016 scores
  was not done.
- The hotspot threshold of 0.6 is relative to the maximum density, and one
  dominant hotspot makes other areas look weak. The kernel bandwidth was kept
  from Trial 5.
- 8 fire points fell outside the 500 m mask and were dropped.
- The Trial 5 pie charts and the Trial 7 pie charts are not comparable. They
  come from different models, different periods and different point designs.
- The class thresholds come from natural breaks on the 500 m mean score and
  depend on the data.
- The class maps in the figures are the smoothed 30 m display surface. The class
  counts and shares come from the 500 m map. Small differences at class borders
  are expected.

  classes are the reference.
- Show Validation_B_Overlay as the main overlay and Validation_A_SideBySide as
  the supporting figure.
- Report the AUC with and without the buffer, and explain in one sentence why
  the buffer flatters the result.
- Report the visual finding honestly: the strongest hotspot is only partly
  covered by the High class. Do not explain it with a cause that was not
  tested. Possible causes to list as open: ignition by people (not described by
  the predictors), land cover and management at that location, and noise in the
  data.
- State that FIRMS was not a training input, and that it comes from the same
  satellite family as the labels.
- State the period used and that confidence 75 or more detections were used,
  without clustering.
- Cite: Giglio, L., Schroeder, W. and Justice, C.O. (2016). The collection 6
  MODIS active fire detection algorithm and fire products. Remote Sensing of
  Environment, 178, 31 to 41. Check the exact reference before use.
