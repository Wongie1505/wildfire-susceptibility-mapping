PHASE 2: STATIC PREDICTOR STACK (TRIAL 7)
=========================================

Status: COMPLETE. Date run: 14 November 2026.

Context. Trial 7 is a declared new analysis. It was started after the Trial 6
test results were known (Random Forest test AUC 0.571), so nothing in Trial 7
may be presented as a blind first run. No model has been trained in Trial 7
and no test result was used for any choice in this phase.


1. PURPOSE
----------
Phase 1 confirmed that every input opens and that the six rasters and the
boundary share EPSG:32736 and the DEM grid (30.6342 m). Phase 2 turns the
static layers into one stack on the 500 m modelling grid, so each 500 m cell
of the forest carries seven predictor values. "Static" means the layer does
not change between fire seasons. Layers that change by season (rainfall,
NDMI, LST, NDVI, fire history, yearly LULC) are built in later phases.


2. WHAT WAS DONE
----------------
- Built a 500 m reference grid over the forest boundary.
- Computed elevation, slope and aspect from the 30.6342 m DEM, then averaged
  them onto the 500 m grid.
- Computed distance to the nearest river, road and settlement from each
  500 m cell centre.
- Masked everything outside the forest boundary.
- Stacked the seven layers into one GeoTIFF and wrote a data dictionary.
- Checked that all seven layers have the same number of valid cells.

The old Trial 6 derived rasters (Viphya_Slope, aspect eastness and northness,
Rivers_Distances, Roads_distances) were used only in the Phase 1 check. They
were not reused. All of them were recomputed on the new grid.


3. HOW IT WAS DONE
------------------
Script: build_static_stack.py (Spyder). Settlement export: gee_settlements.js
(Google Earth Engine).

Step 1. Reference grid.
  CRS from the boundary: EPSG:32736. Cell size 500 m. The origin is snapped to
  multiples of 500 (x0 = floor(xmin / 500) x 500, top edge = ceil(ymax / 500)
  x 500), so every later GEE export can use the identical grid.
  Boundary extent: x 575,116 to 612,839, y 8,648,947 to 8,727,438.
  Expected grid from this extent: 76 columns by 158 rows (12,008 cells),
  origin 575,000 / 8,727,500. Confirm in the layer properties.
  A cell is inside the forest if its centre falls inside the boundary polygon.

Step 2. Elevation, slope, aspect.
  DEM nodata (-32768) was set to NaN first. Slope and aspect were calculated at
  the native 30.6342 m resolution with numpy gradient (central differences),
  then averaged onto the 500 m grid.
    slope = atan( sqrt(dz/dx^2 + dz/dy^2) ), in degrees.
    Aspect is stored as two components of the downslope direction, eastness
    and northness, each between -1 and 1. Flat cells have no direction and
    are NaN.
  Averaging method: GDAL average resampling. Each 500 m cell takes the
  area-weighted mean of the 30.6342 m cells it overlaps, ignoring NaN.
  500 / 30.6342 = 16.32, which is not a whole number, so block aggregation by
  an exact factor was not possible.

Step 3. Distance to rivers and roads.
  rivers.shp and roads.shp were reprojected to EPSG:32736 if needed. For each
  500 m cell centre the script finds the nearest feature (exact nearest
  geometry, STRtree) and records the straight-line distance in metres.

Step 4. Distance to settlements.
  Source: GHSL GHS-BUILT-S, release P2023A, epoch 2000, band built_surface
  (m2 of built surface per 100 m cell). Asset ID:
  JRC/GHSL/P2023A/GHS_BUILT_S/2000.
  Exported from GEE in EPSG:32736 at 100 m for a rectangle about 30 km larger
  than the forest (longitude 33.40 to 34.31, latitude -12.50 to -11.24), so a
  settlement just outside the forest still counts as the nearest one.
  A 100 m cell counts as settlement when built_surface is at least 500 m2.
  Distance = from each 500 m cell centre to the nearest settlement cell
  centre (KD-tree).

Step 5. Mask and save.
  Cells outside the forest were set to NaN, then to the nodata value -9999.
  The seven layers were written as bands of one float32 GeoTIFF.


4. WHY EACH CHOICE WAS MADE
---------------------------
- 500 m cells: the burned-area labels planned for Phase 3 (MODIS MCD64A1) are
  a 500 m product. The model grid has to match the labels.
- DEM as reference: the DEM defines the CRS and the area covered. It cannot
  define the cell size, because 30.6 m is far finer than the labels.
- Origin snapped to 500: GEE exports can reproduce the exact grid without
  manual alignment.
- Slope and aspect at 30 m before averaging: averaging the DEM first would
  smooth hills flat and underestimate slope.
- Aspect as eastness and northness: raw degrees cannot be averaged. 359 and 1
  degrees face almost the same way but average to 180.
- Distances from cell centres: distance is a property of a point, so each cell
  gets the distance from its centre. Averaging distances over 30 m cells would
  add work and no information.
- GHSL epoch 2000: it predates the first training season (2002), so no
  settlement built during the study window leaks into the predictors.
- Settlement threshold 500 m2: stops isolated low-value pixels from counting as
  a village. This value is a default chosen by Claude and not yet confirmed by
  the author (see section 12).
- Old Trial 6 distance and aspect rasters not reused: they came from the old
  30 m workflow and a settlement layer that is being replaced.
- Resampling happens once, here. This follows the Trial 7 rule: resample only
  when aligning layers from different sources, and again right before
  visualization.

8. CHALLENGES AND FIXES
-----------------------
1. Three different nodata values (DEM -32768, aspect -3.4e38, others -9999).
   Fix: all were converted to NaN before any calculation, and the averaging
   ignores NaN, so edge cells are not pulled toward nodata.
2. 500 m is not a whole multiple of 30.6342 m. Fix: area-weighted average
   resampling instead of block aggregation.
3. The nearest settlement can lie outside the forest. Fix: the GEE export
   extends about 30 km beyond the boundary.
4. Confusion over the reference grid (DEM against 500 m). Settled as: the DEM
   gives the CRS and area, the 500 m grid with origin snapped to multiples of
   500 gives cell size and alignment.
5. Script bug found in testing. The first version unpacked the KD-tree result
   in the wrong order, so settlement "distances" came out as index numbers (a
   maximum of 8 where several kilometres were expected). It was caught by
   running the script on dummy data with known geometry, before it was run on
   the real files. After the fix the dummy test returned a maximum of 11,823 m,
   which matches the geometry. The real run used the fixed script.


9. LIMITATIONS
--------------
- Averaging to 500 m removes fine terrain. Mean slope at 500 m is lower than at
  30 m, and steep local features inside a cell are lost.
- Slope uses central differences, not the Horn method used by QGIS and GDAL.
  Values will differ slightly from a QGIS slope map.
- Settlements are a single 2000 snapshot. Growth after 2000 is not represented.
  GHSL may undercount small rural dwellings. This was not checked against
  local data.
- Distances are straight lines, not travel distance along paths or roads.
- Rivers and roads are single-date layers. Their source and date must be
  recorded (section 12).
- Edge cells are in or out by the position of their centre only.
- The mean of eastness and northness inside a cell is shorter than 1 where the
  slope faces mixed directions. This is intended: a value near 0 means no
  consistent direction.


10. WHAT THE DISSERTATION SHOULD NOTE
-------------------------------------
- Suggested wording: "All static predictors were placed on a 500 m grid in
  EPSG:32736, matching the resolution of the MODIS burned-area labels. Slope
  and aspect were computed from the 30.63 m DEM and then averaged onto the
  grid. Aspect was represented by eastness and northness. Distances to rivers,
  roads and settlements were measured from each cell centre."
- State that distance layers are point values at cell centres, while elevation,
  slope and aspect are area means. The statistic differs by layer.
- State that settlements come from GHSL GHS-BUILT-S R2023A, epoch 2000, with a
  500 m2 threshold per 100 m cell, and why 2000 was chosen (before the first
  fire season).
- State that Trial 7 changes the resampling order from Trial 6. Trial 6 put all
  layers on the 30.63 m grid before analysis. Trial 7 aggregates once, to the
  500 m modelling grid.
- Cite: Pesaresi, M. and Politis, P. (2023). GHS-BUILT-S R2023A. European
  Commission, Joint Research Centre.
