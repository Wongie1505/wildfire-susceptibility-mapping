PHASE 4: SEASONAL PREDICTORS (TRIAL 7)
======================================

Status: COMPLETE. Date run: 15 November 2026.

Context. Trial 7 is a declared new analysis, started after the Trial 6 test
results were known (Random Forest test AUC 0.571). No model has been trained
in Trial 7 and no test result was used for any choice in this phase.


1. PURPOSE
----------
Give the model a "when" signal. In Trial 6 the dynamic predictors (NDVI, NDMI,
LST, rainfall) were period medians, so a cell had the same value in every fire
season within a period. The model could learn where fires happen but not which
years were bad. In Trial 7 every cell gets its own value for every season from
2002 to 2024, describing the vegetation and weather in the months before the
fire season starts.

A second problem is also addressed. Absolute period values shifted between
the training and test periods in Trial 6 (mean rainfall 355 in training against
318 in test, mean LST 26.2 against 27.1 degrees). Anomalies put every season on
a common scale relative to a training period normal. They cannot remove a real
climate difference between the periods, only make it visible (section 7).


2. WHAT WAS DONE
----------------
Seven seasonal predictors were built for each of the 23 seasons (161 files).
Five come from Google Earth Engine, two from the Phase 3 burned maps.

  rain_anomaly      CHIRPS rainfall, 1 Nov (Y-1) to 30 Apr (Y), as % of normal
  ndvi_peak         highest MODIS NDVI, 1 Dec (Y-1) to 30 Apr (Y)
  ndmi_anomaly      mean MODIS NDMI, 1 May to 30 Jun (Y), minus normal
  lst_anomaly       mean MODIS day LST in deg C, 1 May to 30 Jun (Y), minus normal
  lulc              MODIS land cover class (IGBP) of the previous year (Y-1)
  burn_count_3yr    number of the previous 3 seasons in which the cell burned
  years_since_burn  years since the cell last burned, capped at 5

Y is the fire season being predicted (the season runs 1 July to 31 December).
Every predictor is measured before 1 July of that season. One extra file,
burned_2001.tif, was exported so that fire history exists for 2002.


3. HOW IT WAS DONE
------------------
Scripts: gee_predictors.js (Earth Engine) and build_predictors.py (Spyder).

Sources:
  CHIRPS daily rainfall        UCSB-CHG/CHIRPS/DAILY, about 5 km
  MODIS MOD09A1 V061           8-day surface reflectance, 500 m
  MODIS MOD11A2 V061           8-day land surface temperature, 1 km
  MODIS MCD12Q1 V061           yearly land cover, band LC_Type1 (IGBP)
  MODIS MCD64A1 V061           monthly burned area (for burned_2001)

Step 1. Rainfall. Daily CHIRPS summed from 1 November of the previous year to
  30 April. Divided by the normal and multiplied by 100, so 100 is an average
  wet season.
Step 2. NDVI and NDMI. From MOD09A1 reflectance scaled by 0.0001.
    NDVI = (NIR - Red) / (NIR + Red), bands 2 and 1.
    NDMI = (NIR - SWIR1) / (NIR + SWIR1), bands 2 and 6.
  Both were clamped to the valid range of -1 to 1.
  NDVI peak uses no cloud mask. Clouds lower NDVI, so the maximum over the
  8-day composites already ignores them.
  NDMI uses a cloud mask (cloud state, cloud shadow, internal cloud flag from
  StateQA). Where no clear observation exists in May and June, the value falls
  back to clear observations from April to June, and as a last resort to May
  and June without the mask.
Step 3. LST. MOD11A2 daytime LST (LST_Day_1km), good and other-quality pixels
  kept (QC_Day bits 0 and 1 at most 1), converted to deg C (x 0.02 - 273.15).
  Mean of May and June.
Step 4. Anomalies. "Normal" is the average of each measure over the training
  years 2002 to 2015 only. NDMI and LST anomalies are the season value minus
  the normal. Rainfall is the season value as a percentage of the normal.
Step 5. Land cover. The map for year Y-1, so the 2010 file holds the 2009 map.
Step 6. Resampling. Everything was exported to the Phase 2 grid (EPSG:32736,
  500 m, same origin) with an explicit crsTransform, using the default nearest
  neighbour resampling.
Step 7. Fire history (Python). From the burned maps: burn_count_3yr sums
  burned seasons in Y-1, Y-2 and Y-3. years_since_burn is the number of years
  back to the most recent burn within 5 seasons, and 5 if there was none.
  burned_2001 comes from GEE, using the same rule as Phase 3. Later seasons
  come from the Phase 3 burned maps.
Step 8. Cleaning and check (Python). Each file is checked against the Phase 2
  grid, cells outside the forest are set to -9999, and predictor_check.csv
  records the empty cells and value range of every file.


4. WHY EACH CHOICE WAS MADE
---------------------------
- Before 1 July: fire changes the satellite signal. A burned cell has a lower
  NDVI and a hotter surface. Measuring inside the fire season would let the
  model recognise burn scars instead of predicting risk.
- Two windows, not one. The predictors answer two different questions:
    Fuel (how much grass and litter grew): the wet season, November to April.
    Dryness (how dry is it just before burning starts): May and June.
  Rain in July to December is mostly zero and says little. The windows are
  called the "fuel window" and the "dryness window".
- Same window for every cell in a season: using each cell's own burn date would
  give burned and unburned cells different kinds of data, and the model could
  learn that difference instead of fire risk.
- Normals from the training years only: if the test years helped define normal,
  that would be a quiet form of peeking.
- Previous-year land cover: the map for year Y could already be shaped by that
  year's fires.
- Fire history: recent burns remove fuel, long unburned cells carry more. It is
  widely found to be a strong fire predictor and uses only past seasons.
- Nearest neighbour for CHIRPS and LST: it keeps the original values. It adds
  no detail, and is used only to put everything on one grid.
- No cloud mask for the NDVI peak, and fallbacks for NDMI: see section 8.

  ndmi_anomaly      -0.374 to 0.400. Training 0.000, test -0.005. Yearly means

  lulc              IGBP classes 1 to 12.
  burn_count_3yr    0 to 3, mean 0.41.
  years_since_burn  1 to 5, mean 3.90.

First-run console check (before the NDVI and NDMI fix), mean over the forest:
  2010: rain 113.6, NDVI peak 0.816, NDMI anomaly 0.012, LST anomaly 0.04
  2020: rain 108.1, NDVI peak 0.834, NDMI anomaly -0.027, LST anomaly 0.99

Reading the result: the values are in plausible ranges and the anomalies are
centred where they should be. NDVI peak and NDMI show almost no shift between
training and test. LST is 0.27 deg C warmer in the test period (the Trial 6
absolute means differed by about 0.9 deg C). Rainfall is the one real shift:
the test period is about 10% wetter than the training normal. Trial 6 rainfall
was defined differently, so the two rainfall shifts are not directly
comparable. The model will meet wetter test seasons than it was trained on,
and this should be stated as a limitation.


8. CHALLENGES AND FIXES
-----------------------
1. Gaps in the first export. The first Python check found 25 files with empty
   forest cells, 4,102 empty values in total. All were in two predictors:
     ndvi_peak: 17 files, up to 884 cells (14% of the forest) in 2014, then
                488 in 2011 and 439 in 2020.
     ndmi_anomaly: 8 files, up to 429 cells in 2005.
   Cause: the cloud mask was too strict, especially in the cloudy rainy season
   that the NDVI window covers. A cell with no value cannot be used in the
   model.
   Fix: NDVI peak now uses the unmasked composites, since the maximum already
   skips clouds. NDMI falls back to a wider window and then to unmasked data.
   Both NDVI and NDMI exports were redone (46 files) because the NDMI normal
   changed. The second check showed 0 empty cells in all 161 files.
2. NDVI above 1. The first NDVI peak reached 1.044, which is impossible, caused
   by slightly negative reflectance. Fixed by clamping to -1 to 1. The maximum
   is now exactly 1.000, so some cells sit at the clamp (section 12).
3. Number of files. 116 exports would be too many to start by hand. They were
   started with the Run all button in the Code Editor.
4. Resolution mismatch. CHIRPS is about 5 km and LST is 1 km, while the grid is
   500 m. Nearest neighbour was accepted as a way to align them, knowing it
   adds no information.


9. LIMITATIONS
--------------
- CHIRPS and LST have coarse native resolution. Neighbouring 500 m cells share
  the same value, so these predictors say little about local differences.
- Some NDMI cells use the wider window or unmasked data. The number of such
  cells was not counted.
- NDVI saturates in dense forest. Its range is narrow (yearly means 0.81 to
  0.86), so it may separate cells poorly.
- Fire history is incomplete in the first seasons. The record starts in 2001,
  so burn_count_3yr is incomplete for 2002 and 2003, and years_since_burn is
  incomplete for 2002 to 2005. A cell that burned earlier looks "never burned".
- Fire history comes from the MCD64A1 labels, so missed burns carry into the
  predictors. It can also dominate the model, which must be checked in
  Phase 7.
- Anomalies are measured against a training period normal. In the wetter,
  warmer test period this is intended, and the shift is reported openly.
- Land cover is a yearly classification, and a class can change between years
  because of classification noise, not real change.
- All cells in a season share one measurement window, not each cell's burn
  date.
