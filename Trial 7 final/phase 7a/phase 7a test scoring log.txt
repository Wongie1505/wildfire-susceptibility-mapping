PHASE 7a: TEST SCORING AND IMPORTANCE (TRIAL 7)
===============================================

Status: COMPLETE. Date run: 15 November 2026.
Model scored: the 6a Random Forest (both fire history predictors), trained on
2002 to 2015 only. It was scored once, on 2016 to 2024.

Context. Trial 7 is a declared new analysis, started after the Trial 6 test
results were known (Random Forest test AUC 0.571). The model and its settings
were fixed in Phase 6, before this phase. No change was made to the model
after the test score was seen.


1. PURPOSE
----------
Measure how well the final model predicts fire in years it has never seen, and
find out which predictors it relies on. This is the headline result of Trial 7.


2. WHAT WAS DONE
----------------
- Loaded the saved 6a model and the 56,754 test rows (9 seasons x 6,306 cells).
- Scored every test row once.
- Calculated three measures: pooled AUC, AUC for each test year, and the "top
  20% capture" rate.
- Measured predictor importance by grouped permutation importance. The two fire
  history predictors were shuffled together as one group.
- Saved two figures and the predictions for later use.


3. HOW IT WAS DONE
------------------
Script: phase7a_test_score.py (Spyder).

Safeguard. The script refuses to run if test_results.csv already exists, so the
test years cannot be scored twice by accident. A second run would need the file
to be deleted on purpose, and would have to be disclosed.

Step 1. Load. Only rows with split = test were kept. The model expects the 14
  predictors in the order used in Phase 6, and the script stops if the count
  differs.
Step 2. Score. The model gives each cell a score between 0 and 1, which is
  the predicted share of trees voting for fire. It is a relative susceptibility
  score, not a calibrated probability.
Step 3. Measures.
    Pooled AUC: all 56,754 rows together. This is the same kind of number as the
    Trial 6 test AUC (0.571).
    AUC by year: each season scored on its own.
    Top 20% capture: in each season, the cells are ranked by score. The measure
    is the share of that season's burned cells that fall in the highest-scored
    fifth of cells. Chance gives 20%.
Step 4. Grouped permutation importance. One predictor at a time, or the two
  fire history predictors together, is shuffled across the test rows and the
  drop in pooled AUC is recorded. 5 repeats, seed 42. This step does not change
  the model.
Step 5. Figures at 300 dpi: AUC by year with the pooled value, the Trial 6
  value and the chance line, and a bar chart of the AUC drops.


4. WHY EACH CHOICE WAS MADE
---------------------------
- Scored once, with a lock: a test set is only a fair test if it is looked at
  once. Repeated looks turn it into a second tuning set.
- Pooled AUC and AUC by year: the pooled value allows comparison with Trial 6.
  The yearly values show how skill changes between seasons. They do not hide a
  weak year inside an average.
- Top 20% capture: AUC is hard to explain, while "how many of the burned cells
  fall in the top fifth of the map" is easy to explain and easy to check
  against chance.
- Grouped permutation importance: the two fire history predictors carry nearly
  the same information (rho -0.945). Shuffled one at a time, each would look
  weak, because the other covers for it. Shuffled together they show the real
  weight of fire history.
- Importance measured on the test rows after the score was fixed: it describes
  what the model uses on unseen data and leads to no change in the model.

7. RESULTS
----------
Test rows: 56,754 (9 seasons x 6,306 cells), 8,774 burned (15.46%).

Pooled test AUC: 0.6029. Trial 6 test AUC for the Random Forest: 0.571.

Results by year (test_results.csv):

  Year   Burned %   AUC      Top 20% capture %
  2016   30.2       0.6377   30.9
  2017    8.2       0.7008   45.0
  2018   11.5       0.6817   33.8
  2019   11.2       0.6486   36.2
  2020    9.6       0.6153   34.0
  2021   18.8       0.6116   30.1
  2022   15.2       0.5831   25.1
  2023   18.7       0.5579   26.4
  2024   15.8       0.5521   25.3
  All    15.5       0.6029   31.9 (mean of the nine years)

Summaries:
- Mean of the nine yearly AUC values: 0.621. The pooled AUC (0.603) is lower.
- First three test years (2016 to 2018) average 0.673. Last three (2022 to
  2024) average 0.564.
- Top 20% capture averages 31.9% against 20% by chance. It is 45% in 2017 and
  about 25% in 2022 to 2024.

Grouped permutation importance (AUC drop when shuffled, mean and SD of 5):
  fire history (both predictors)  0.0554 (0.0010)
  land cover                      0.0138 (0.0005)
  distance to rivers              0.0101 (0.0009)
  distance to roads               0.0093 (0.0012)
  distance to settlements         0.0044 (0.0003)
  All other predictors are below 0.0044. See importance_grouped.csv.

Reference points from Phase 6 (training years only):
  Spatial-block CV AUC 0.759. Forward chaining AUC 0.6615 (2009 to 2015).

Reading the result:
- The model is better than Trial 6 (0.603 against 0.571), a gain of 0.032, but
  the gain is modest.
- Skill falls with distance from the training period. It is highest in 2017 and
  lowest in 2023 and 2024. The decline is the main finding of this phase.
- The pooled AUC is below the average yearly AUC. The model ranks cells within
  a year better than it ranks the seasons against each other. For instance
  30.2% of cells burned in 2016, a heavy year, and 8.2% in 2017, a light year.
  The lower pooled value suggests the model did not separate heavy and light
  years well. This was not checked directly.
- The forward chaining estimate (0.66) was higher than the test result (0.60).
  A likely reason is that forward chaining only looked one year ahead from the
  training years, while the test looks up to nine years ahead.
- The model relies mostly on where and what the land is (fire history, land
  cover, distance to rivers and roads). The seasonal climate predictors add
  little, as the single-predictor check in Phase 5 suggested.


8. CHALLENGES AND FIXES
-----------------------
No technical problems. The script ran as written. The result fell short of the
personal target of 0.65 set at the start of the work, and was reported as it
came out. No setting was changed in response.


9. LIMITATIONS
--------------
- The cause of the decline over time is not proven. Candidates are a change in
  fire patterns, the 2000 settlement layer going out of date, the wetter test
  period (test rainfall about 10% above the training normal) and noise in the
  burned area labels. This phase cannot separate them.
- No confidence interval was calculated for the test AUC. With nine seasons the
  uncertainty is large, and a difference of 0.03 from Trial 6 should be called
  modest.
- Trial 6 and Trial 7 use different labels and different row designs, so the
  two AUC values are not a strict like-for-like comparison.
- The test years 2016 to 2024 were already scored in Trial 6, so they are not a
  blind test in the strict sense.
- Permutation importance was measured on the test rows. It describes what the
  model uses on unseen data, but it is not independent of the test score.
- Shuffled predictors that are correlated with others (distances to rivers and
  roads, elevation, slope) can share importance in ways this method cannot
  separate.
- Top 20% capture for "All" is the mean of the yearly values, not a pooled
  value.

  the yearly values in the figure. Say what AUC means: the chance that a
  randomly chosen burned cell scores higher than a randomly chosen unburned
  cell in the same season.
- Report top 20% capture (31.9% against 20% by chance), with the yearly range.
- Report the decline over time as a finding and say that its cause could not be
  determined here.
- Report that the model was scored once, that the settings were fixed in Phase
  6, and that no change followed the test score.
- Show the importance figure. Explain why the fire history pair was permuted
  together. State that the seasonal climate predictors contribute little.
- Report that forward chaining (0.66) overstated test performance (0.60).
- If the proposal states a target for model performance, report the result
  against that target and explain the gap.
- Cite:
