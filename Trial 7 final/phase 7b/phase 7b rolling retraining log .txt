PHASE 7b: ROLLING RETRAINING, POST-TEST EXTENSION (TRIAL 7)
===========================================================

Status: COMPLETE. Date run: 15 November 2026.

IMPORTANT. This phase was decided AFTER the test score of Phase 7a was known
(decision T7-35). It is a diagnostic reported next to the headline result. It
is not a blind test and it does not replace the headline result. The headline
stays the static 6a model: pooled test AUC 0.6029.


1. PURPOSE
----------
Phase 7a showed that the model's skill fades over the test years (AUC 0.70 in
2017 and 0.55 in 2024). One possible cause is that the model, trained once on
2002 to 2015, goes out of date. This phase tests that explanation. If the
fade is caused by staleness, a model that is retrained every year should hold
its skill.


2. WHAT WAS DONE
----------------
- For each test year from 2016 to 2024, trained a new Random Forest on every
  earlier year and scored that year.
- Compared the result, year by year, with the static model from Phase 7a.
- Ran a supporting check on whether the quality of the burned area labels
  changed between the training and test periods, using the Phase 3 table of
  agreement with FIRMS fire events.


3. HOW IT WAS DONE
------------------
Script: phase7b_rolling_retrain.py (Spyder).

Settings. The 6a winning settings, unchanged: max_depth 14, min_samples_leaf
30, max_features 0.5, max_samples 0.5. 300 trees, class_weight
balanced_subsample, seed 42. Nothing was tuned again.

Rolling retraining (expanding window). For each test year t:
  Training rows: every row with year before t. This includes the earlier test
  years, because their fire outcomes would already be known in real use.
  The training set grows from 88,284 rows (for 2016, identical to the static
  model) to 138,732 rows (for 2024), by 6,306 rows per year.
  Validation: the 6,306 cells of year t.
Measures: AUC for each year, pooled AUC over the nine rolling predictions, and
top 20% capture (the share of that year's burned cells that fall in the
highest-scored fifth of cells). The static values come from the Phase 7a file
test_results.csv.

Safeguards. The script uses half of the available cores and refuses to run if
rolling_results.csv already exists.

Supporting label check. The Phase 3 file firms_agreement.csv gives, for each
season, the share of FIRMS fire events that fall in a burned cell. The mean of
that share was compared between 2002 to 2015 and 2016 to 2024. This check does
not use the model.


4. WHY EACH CHOICE WAS MADE
---------------------------
- Expanding window: it keeps all information and copies how an early warning
  system would use last year's fires.
- Same settings as 6a: the question is whether retraining helps, not whether
  retuning helps. Retuning on the test years would invalidate the result.
- Reported next to the headline: the rolling model sees earlier test labels, so
  it is not a blind test. It is a forecast simulation and is labelled as one.
- FIRMS agreement as a label check: if the fade came from worsening labels, the
  agreement should fall over time. It is the only independent check of the
  labels available in the project.

Supporting label check (Phase 3 firms_agreement.csv):
  Share of FIRMS events inside a burned cell, mean of the seasons:
    2002 to 2015: 24.9%        2016 to 2024: 27.4%
  Share within one cell of a burn:
    2002 to 2015: 50.2%        2016 to 2024: 58.9%
  Agreement divided by the chance level (the burned share of cells):
    2002 to 2015: 1.89         2016 to 2024: 1.90
  In the test years the share in a burned cell was 22.6, 26.4, 23.6, 17.0,
  27.5, 30.4, 32.7 and 24.7% for 2017 to 2024, with no downward trend.

Reading the result:
- Yearly retraining helps a little. The pooled AUC rises from 0.603 to 0.612
  and the mean yearly AUC from 0.621 to 0.631.
- Most single-year differences are 0.01 or smaller, within noise. The visible
  gains are in 2022 (+0.033) and 2023 (+0.027). The first three years are
  unchanged.
- The fade remains. The rolling model falls from 0.713 in 2017 to 0.566 in
  2024. A model going out of date explains only a small part of the decline.
- The label check does not show worsening labels. Agreement with FIRMS is about
  the same in both periods, and no downward trend appears across the test
  years.
- The cause of the decline therefore lies elsewhere. It was not identified.


8. CHALLENGES AND FIXES
-----------------------
No technical problems. The nine refits ran as written. The label check was
added after the rolling result, as a supporting step, and is marked as such.


9. LIMITATIONS
--------------
- Decided after the test score was known. This is a post-test diagnostic.
- The rolling model is trained on earlier test years, so its scores are not a
  blind test. They are a forecast simulation.
- The gain of 0.009 (pooled) and 0.010 (mean yearly) is small. No confidence
  interval was calculated, and with nine seasons the uncertainty is large.
- The settings were tuned for 88,284 training rows and were not tuned again for
  the larger training sets.
- Only an expanding window was tried. A sliding window that drops the oldest
  years was not tested.
- The FIRMS check covers one aspect of label quality. It would not detect a
  steady under-detection of burns by MCD64A1 that affects both periods alike.
- Remaining candidates for the decline are changes in fire drivers that the
  predictors do not describe (for example fire management or ignition
  behaviour), an out-of-date settlement layer (GHSL epoch 2000), and the wetter
  test period (rainfall about 10% above the training normal). The data cannot
  separate them.


10. WHAT THE DISSERTATION SHOULD NOTE
-------------------------------------
- Present this as a diagnostic that followed the test result, and say so in
  plain words. Report the static result as the headline.
- Suggested wording: "To test whether the decline in skill over 2016 to 2024
  reflected a model going out of date, the model was retrained each year on all
  earlier years (rolling-origin evaluation). Retraining raised the mean yearly
  AUC from 0.621 to 0.631 and the pooled AUC from 0.603 to 0.612, but the
  decline persisted, so model age explains only a small part of it."
- Report that the agreement between burned area and FIRMS did not worsen over
  time, which argues against label degradation as the main cause.
- State that the cause of the decline remains open, and list the candidates in
  the limitations. Do not claim one of them.
- Show rolling_vs_static.png.
- Cite: Tashman, L.J. (2000). Out-of-sample tests of forecasting accuracy: an
  analysis and review. International Journal of Forecasting, 16, 437 to 450.
  Check the exact reference before use.
