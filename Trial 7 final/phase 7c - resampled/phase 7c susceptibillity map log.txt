PHASE 7c: SUSCEPTIBILITY MAP, CLASS AREAS AND MAP CHECKS (TRIAL 7)
==================================================================

Status: COMPLETE. Date run: 15 November 2026.

Context. Trial 7 is a declared new analysis. No model was run in this phase.
The map is built from the predictions saved in Phase 7a.


1. PURPOSE
----------
Turn the model's scores into the map product of the dissertation: a wildfire
susceptibility map of the Chikangawa forest, with class areas, and check that
the map ranks places in the right order.


2. WHAT WAS DONE
----------------
- Averaged the 6a model scores of each 500 m cell over the nine test seasons
  (2016 to 2024).
- Divided the cells into five classes, very low to very high, by natural
  breaks.
- Wrote the mean score map and the class map as GeoTIFFs on the Phase 2 grid.
- Calculated the area of each class in hectares and square kilometres.
- Checked each class against the fires that actually occurred in 2016 to 2024.
- Calculated two overall measures of agreement between the map and the burns.


3. HOW IT WAS DONE
------------------
Script: phase7c_susceptibility_map.py (Spyder).

Step 1. Mean score. The file test_predictions.csv from Phase 7a holds a score
  for every cell and test season. For each cell the nine scores were averaged
  (6,306 cells, 9 seasons). The number of seasons each cell burned (0 to 9) was
  also counted.
Step 2. Grid. Cell positions come from cell_id (row x width + column) and the
  Phase 2 stack. The script stops if a scored cell lies outside the forest.
Step 3. Classes. Fisher-Jenks natural breaks on the 6,306 mean scores, five
  classes. A cell takes the higher class if its score is equal to or above a
  threshold. The thresholds are saved in class_breaks.csv.
Step 4. Rasters. Float32 mean score and int16 class (1 = very low, 5 = very
  high), nodata -9999, same grid as static_stack.tif.
Step 5. Areas. One cell is 25 ha (500 m x 500 m, 0.25 km2).
Step 6. Class check. For each class: the share of cell-seasons that burned (the
  cells of the class x 9 seasons), the ratio of that share to the overall share,
  and the share of its cells that burned at least once.
Step 7. Overall agreement. Spearman rank correlation between mean score and the
  number of seasons burned, and the AUC of the mean score for "burned at least
  once in 2016 to 2024".
Step 8. Two figures at 300 dpi: a map and a bar chart of the burned share by
  class.


4. WHY EACH CHOICE WAS MADE
---------------------------
- Average of the test seasons: one season is noisy, and the average is a stable
  picture of where fire is likely. The test seasons were not used to train the
  model, so the map is out of sample for the model itself.
- Not the 23-season average: 14 of those seasons are training years. The model
  has seen their fires, and the map would partly repeat history.
- Natural breaks, not equal counts: equal-count classes give every class the
  same area by construction, which makes the area table empty of information.
  Natural breaks follow the gaps in the scores.
- Five classes: a common number in susceptibility mapping, and readable.
- Class check on burned share: it shows directly whether a higher class burns
  more often, in a form that is easy to read.
- Scores are used as relative susceptibility, not as probabilities (D21).

  5      Very high  1,039    25,975      259.75       16.5%

  Class      Burned cell-seasons   Burned share   Ratio to overall   Cells burned

  Very low     177                  3.4%           0.22               24.0%
  Low          854                  8.4%           0.54               49.8%
  Moderate   1,861                 15.0%           0.97               68.9%

  Overall burned share of cell-seasons: 15.5%. The total of burned cell-seasons
  is 8,774, as in Phase 5.

Overall agreement (map_summary.csv):
  Spearman, mean score against number of seasons burned:  0.417
  AUC, mean score against burned at least once:           0.712

Reading the result:
- The classes are in the right order. The burned share rises in every step, from
  3.4% in the very low class to 24.9% in the very high class, which is about
  7.3 times as often.
- The high and very high classes cover 51.0% of the forest and hold 67.0% of
  all burned cell-seasons. The very high class alone covers 16.5% of the forest
  and holds 26.5% of the burns.
- The moderate class burned almost exactly at the overall rate (ratio 0.97).
- Spearman 0.417: cells that the map ranks higher tend to burn in more seasons.
  The link is moderate, not tight. A value of 0 would mean no link and 1 would
  mean a perfect match in ranking. A perfect value is not expected, because fire
  has a strong random element, and most cells have only a few burned seasons,
  which limits how high the correlation can go.
- AUC 0.712: if one cell that burned at least once and one cell that never
  burned are picked at random, the map gives the burned cell the higher score
  71% of the time. This is higher than the test AUC of 0.603 because it asks an
  easier question: whether a cell burns at all in nine years, not whether it
  burns in one particular season, and averaging nine seasons removes noise. The
  two AUC values are not comparable and must not be presented as an improvement
  in model skill.
- The high class is the largest (34.5% of the forest). Half of the forest is in
  the high or very high class, so the map separates areas well but is not very
  selective for targeting action.


8. CHALLENGES AND FIXES
-----------------------
No technical problems. The script ran as written. The main concern is
methodological and is described in section 9 (overlap of fire history with the
check).


9. LIMITATIONS
--------------
- Overlap between the model inputs and the check. The fire history predictors
  of a season use the burns of the previous five seasons. For test seasons that
  includes earlier test seasons. The map check counts burns in the same 2016 to
  2024 window. A cell that burned in 2016 therefore carries that fact into its
  scores for later seasons, and then scores well against the same burn. The
  class ranking, the Spearman value and the AUC of 0.712 are partly inflated by
  this overlap. The size of the effect was not measured. A cleaner check would
  use a map built only from information available before the check window (for
  example the 2016 scores only, checked against the burns of 2017 to 2024).
- The map reflects the conditions of 2016 to 2024 only, and the model's skill
  fell over those years (Phase 7a).
- Scores are relative susceptibility and not probabilities of fire.
- Class thresholds depend on the data. Another set of seasons would give other
  breaks and other areas.
- Natural breaks make the high class very large. A stricter scheme would give a
  more selective map.
- 500 m cells, and coarse rainfall and temperature inputs, limit how fine the
  map can be.
- The burned area labels miss some small and under-canopy burns (Phase 3), so
  the burned shares per class are lower bounds.
- The test years were already scored in Trial 6, so they are not blind in the
  strict sense.
