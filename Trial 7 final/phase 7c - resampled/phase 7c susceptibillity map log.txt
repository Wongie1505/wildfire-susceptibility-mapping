PHASE 7c: SUSCEPTIBILITY MAP, CLASS AREAS AND MAP CHECKS (TRIAL 7)
==================================================================

Status: COMPLETE. Date run: 15 November 2026.
Two runs: a first run with five classes, then a final run with three classes
and the Trial 5 map design. The final run is the one reported.

Context. Trial 7 is a declared new analysis. No model was run in this phase.
The map is built from the predictions saved in Phase 7a.


1. PURPOSE
----------
Turn the model's scores into the map product of the dissertation: a wildfire
susceptibility map of the Chikangawa forest, with class areas, and check that
the map ranks places in the right order.


2. WHAT WAS DONE
----------------
- Averaged the 6a model scores of each 500 m cell over the nine test seasons
  (2016 to 2024).
- Divided the cells into classes by natural breaks: five classes in the first
  run, three (Low, Medium, High) in the final run.
- Wrote the mean score and class maps as GeoTIFFs on the 500 m model grid.
- Smoothed the map onto the 30.6 m DEM grid for display and wrote a 30 m score
  and a 30 m class GeoTIFF.
- Calculated the area of each class in hectares and square kilometres, on the
  500 m grid.
- Checked each class against the fires that actually occurred in 2016 to 2024.
- Calculated two overall measures of agreement between the map and the burns.
- Built the final two-panel figure in the design of the Trial 5 map.


3. HOW IT WAS DONE
------------------
Script: phase7c_susceptibility_map.py (Spyder).

Step 1. Mean score. The file test_predictions.csv from Phase 7a holds a score
  for every cell and test season. For each cell the nine scores were averaged
  (6,306 cells, 9 seasons). The number of seasons each cell burned (0 to 9) was
  also counted.
Step 2. Classes. Fisher-Jenks natural breaks on the 6,306 mean scores. A cell
  takes the higher class if its score equals or exceeds a threshold. The
  thresholds are saved in class_breaks.csv.
Step 3. 500 m rasters (the analysis version): mean score and class, on the
  Phase 2 grid. All areas and all checks use this version.
Step 4. Display surface. The 500 m score map was resampled onto the DEM grid
  (30.6342 m) by bilinear interpolation, so each 30 m pixel blends the nearest
  500 m cell values. Forest pixels the blend could not reach were filled with
  the nearest valid value. The surface was clipped to the forest boundary
  polygon and lightly smoothed (Gaussian, 8 pixels, about 245 m, computed
  inside the forest only) to remove single-cell speckle. The thresholds found
  on the 500 m map were then applied to the smooth surface to give the 30 m
  class map.
Step 5. Class check on the 500 m map. For each class: the share of cell-seasons
  that burned, the ratio of that share to the overall share, and the share of
  cells that burned at least once.
Step 6. Overall agreement on the 500 m map: Spearman rank correlation between
  mean score and the number of seasons burned, and the AUC of the mean score
  for "burned at least once in 2016 to 2024".
Step 7. Figure. Two panels in the Trial 5 design: continuous scores on the left
  with a colour bar, three classes on the right with a legend box, north
  arrows, and a scale bar that is true to the map scale.


4. WHY EACH CHOICE WAS MADE
---------------------------
- Average of the test seasons: one season is noisy, and the average is a stable
  picture of where fire is likely. The test seasons were not used to train the
  model, so the map is out of sample for the model itself.
- Not the 23-season average: 14 of those seasons are training years. The model
  has seen their fires, and the map would partly repeat history.
- Natural breaks, not equal counts or fixed thirds: equal counts give every
  class the same area, so the area table says nothing. Fixed thirds (0.33 and
  0.66) would leave the High class almost empty, because the scores sit between
  about 0.1 and 0.75. Natural breaks follow the gaps in the scores.
- Three classes in the final run: this follows the Trial 5 map design and
  reads easily. The first run used five classes and gave High plus Very high
  as half of the forest.
- Resampling to 30.6 m for display only: the 500 m cells look blocky in a
  figure. Interpolation gives smooth shapes and smooth class borders, and the
  DEM grid makes the surface line up with the other layers. It adds no
  information and no accuracy.
- Areas and checks on the 500 m map: the 30 m surface is interpolated, and any
  area or accuracy figure taken from it would claim detail the model does not
  have.
- Colour scale pinned to the classes: the first final-design figure used a
  fixed 0 to 1 colour bar, and the scores only cover about 0.1 to 0.75, so the
  continuous panel looked yellow and orange while the class panel showed bright
  red. The two panels looked like different maps. The continuous colours now
  pass through the class colours (green, yellow, red) at the middle of each
  class and change at the class thresholds, which are marked on the colour bar.
- Colour bar shows the real score range instead of 0 to 1, for the same reason.
- Colour bar title "Susceptibility score" instead of "Fire probability": the
  scores come from a Random Forest with class weights and are not calibrated
  probabilities (D21).
- The legend slides to the nearest free place so that it never covers the
  forest.

  24.9% (about 7.3 times). High plus Very high covered 51.0% of the forest and
  held 67.0% of the burned cell-seasons.

Reading the result:
- Spearman 0.417: cells that the map ranks higher tend to burn in more seasons.
  The link is moderate, not tight. 0 would mean no link and 1 a perfect match
  in ranking. A perfect value is not expected, because fire has a strong random
  element, and most cells have only a few burned seasons, which limits how high
  the value can go.
- AUC 0.712: if one cell that burned at least once and one cell that never
  burned are picked at random, the map gives the burned cell the higher score
  71% of the time. This is higher than the test AUC of 0.603 because it asks an
  easier question: whether a cell burns at all in nine years, not whether it
  burns in one particular season, and averaging nine seasons removes noise. The
  two AUC values are not comparable and must not be presented as an
  improvement in model skill.
- The three-class results above are to be read in the same way: the burned
  share should rise from Low to High.


8. CHALLENGES AND FIXES
-----------------------
1. The two panels of the first final-design figure looked like different maps.
   The continuous panel used a fixed 0 to 1 colour bar for scores between about
   0.1 and 0.75, so most of it looked yellow or orange, while the class panel
   showed bright red wherever the score passed the High threshold. Fix: the
   colour scale now covers the real range and is pinned to the class colours
   and thresholds (section 4).
2. Layout problems in the first figure: the right-hand title ran into the map,
   the legend covered part of the forest and its heading overflowed the box, and
   the rotated colour bar title overlapped the tick labels. Fix: smaller text
   aligned to the frame edge, a legend that moves to the nearest free place, and
   the colour bar title moved clear of the ticks.
3. Visual speckle in the 30 m class map, from small isolated cell differences.
   Fix: light smoothing of the display surface (section 3, step 4).


9. LIMITATIONS
--------------
- Overlap between the model inputs and the check. The fire history predictors
  of a season use the burns of the previous five seasons. For test seasons that
  includes earlier test seasons. The map check counts burns in the same 2016 to
  2024 window. A cell that burned in 2016 therefore carries that fact into its
  scores for later seasons, and then scores well against the same burn. The
  class ranking, the Spearman value and the AUC of 0.712 are partly inflated by
  this overlap. The size of the effect was not measured. A cleaner check would
  use a map built only from information available before the check window (for
  example the 2016 scores only, checked against the burns of 2017 to 2024).
- The 30 m map is interpolated and smoothed. Its fine detail is not
  information from the model. Areas, class shares and checks must come from the
  500 m version.
- The map reflects the conditions of 2016 to 2024 only, and the model's skill
  fell over those years (Phase 7a).
- Scores are relative susceptibility and not probabilities of fire.
- Class thresholds depend on the data. Another set of seasons would give other
  breaks and other areas.
- The colour scale is tied to this map's thresholds, so colours cannot be
  compared with maps from other models.
- 500 m cells, and coarse rainfall and temperature inputs, limit how fine the
  map can be.
- The burned area labels miss some small and under-canopy burns (Phase 3), so
  the burned shares per class are lower bounds.
- The test years were already scored in Trial 6, so they are not blind in the
  strict sense.
